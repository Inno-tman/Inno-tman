Global IT Trends: Negative Applications Across Global & South African Contexts

Introduction

Emerging technologies manifest differently across jurisdictions: global-scale platforms shape public discourse, while local socio-economic dynamics determine how harms are amplified and experienced. The following compares examples from global settings with South African contexts.

Structured Comparison

TrendGlobal ExampleSouth African / Local Example
Algorithmic Data Mining Cambridge Analytica: harvesting ~50m Facebook profiles to target voters (Scott, 2018). Local digital influence operations adapt targeting to local languages and fault-lines (Allen, 2023).
Social Media Amplification Platform-level accountability failures and repeated public apologies (Tufecki, 2018). Online incitement contributed to the July 2021 Zuma unrest, illustrating rapid translation to offline harm (BBP Law Attorneys, 2025).
Generative AI & Scams AI-enabled fake college website networks facilitating scams and fraud (Moody and Palmer, 2025). Automated astroturfing and targeted misinformation operations tailoring narratives to local electorates (Allen, 2023).

Mitigation Strategies

  • Algorithmic auditing and transparency obligations.
  • Data minimization and strict consent regimes.
  • Public media literacy and localised counter-messaging.

Discussion

Comparisons emphasise that mitigation cannot be one-size-fits-all: platform reforms must consider local governance capacity, legal recourse, and socio-economic vulnerabilities. Cross-border harms require coordination between global platforms and local institutions to reduce asymmetrical risks.
