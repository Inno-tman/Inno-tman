Topic 1 Reflection: The Dark Side of Technology & Non-Neutrality

A concise critical reflection on philosophical framing and three harmful IT trends.

Philosophical Framework: Amoral Tooling vs. Non-Neutral Architecture

John Abell’s oft-cited claim that "technology is amoral" frames tools as value-neutral instruments. Critically, contemporary technology studies argue otherwise: platform architectures embed developer choices, business incentives, and governance trade-offs that make systems non-neutral in practice.

"technology is amoral..." — John Abell (contextual claim)

Platform affordances, monetization models and scale incentives shape outcomes—for example, growth-first product decisions can deprioritize safety and amplify harms (Tufecki, 2018).

In-text citation: (Tufecki, 2018)

Corporate Priorities & Safety Trade-offs

High-level corporate choices—such as growth-at-all-costs—are empirically linked to repeated public apologies and incremental fixes rather than systemic redesigns (Tufecki, 2018). Similarly, platforms that prioritise high-earning creators have sometimes tolerated misinformation because of revenue incentives (Associated Press, 2022).

In-text citations: (Tufecki, 2018; Associated Press, 2022)

Evaluation of Three Key IT Trends

Trend 1: Algorithmic Microtargeting & Data Mining

Whistleblower evidence shows large-scale harvesting of personal profiles to enable psychographic targeting in political campaigns (Scott, 2018). These techniques convert behavioral data into manipulable influence vectors.

In-text citation: (Scott, 2018)

Trend 2: Social Media & Group Messaging

Group messaging and platform amplification can turn online rhetoric into offline harm; legal and policy scrutiny following South Africa's July 2021 unrest highlights how online incitement interacts with local fault-lines (BBP Law Attorneys, 2025; Allen, 2023).

In-text citations: (BBP Law Attorneys, 2025; Allen, 2023)

Trend 3: Generative AI & Synthetic Scams

Generative AI lowers the cost of creating convincing synthetic assets. Recent reporting documents AI-enabled fake college website networks used in financial scams (Moody and Palmer, 2025). Jurisprudence such as Manuel v EFF illustrates emerging legal responses to fabricated content (Milo, 2019).

In-text citations: (Moody and Palmer, 2025; Milo, 2019)
